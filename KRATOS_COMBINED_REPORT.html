How the three parts work as one

A visitor is never handed off to a different system. The record follows them.

Before this, an enquiry arrived by email or form and its life depended on someone noticing it. Nobody could say which ad produced it or who followed up. Now every enquiry is recorded, tagged with where it came from, handed to a named rep within seconds, and visible on a board until it is closed.

What each part does

The website

Live 1 Jun – 10 Aug · 65 releases

Brings buyers in and warms them up before anyone picks up the phone.

  • 31 pages covering residential, commercial, batteries, EV charging and finance.
  • Six calculators — rebate by postcode, battery rebate, solar output, feed-in tariff, EV cost, savings and payback — using official Clean Energy Regulator figures.
  • Built to rank: sitemap, page descriptions, structured data, and pages generated automatically for each state's rebates.
  • Every form, product, image and blog post is edited in the CRM, not by a developer.
  • Runs on your server behind Cloudflare. No platform fee, no third party holding the site.

The CRM

Live 29 Jun – 11 Aug · 47 releases

Owns the enquiry from the moment it arrives until it is won or lost.

  • Forms, landing pages, social and the chatbot all feed one lead list. Duplicates merge instead of multiplying.
  • Every lead permanently records the ad, campaign and page it came from. Campaigns carry their cost, so cost-per-lead is measured, not guessed.
  • New leads distribute round-robin across the team, balanced by workload and office, with instant notification to the rep.
  • Drag-and-drop sales board: lead → deal with priced line items → quoted → negotiation → won or lost. Prices lock at the moment of sale.
  • Four roles with separate access, a full audit trail, and spreadsheet import/export with validation.

The chatbot

Live 8 Jun – 30 Jul

Works the night shift, and hands over to a human when it matters.

  • Answers only from your own uploaded documents — no invented answers — and remembers the conversation.
  • Asks for name, phone and email after a few messages, and politely asks again if the visitor skips.
  • When someone shows buying interest, or the bot has no answer, it shows the sales number and contact form immediately.
  • Staff watch live chats and take over mid-conversation from the CRM, then hand back to the bot.
  • Built to run many separate chatbots on one system — each client sealed off from the others.

The changes that matter

Five differences in kind, not degree.

Nothing lives in a personal inbox

Website, chat, campaign pages and social all land in the same list, on the same record. Leads no longer go cold quietly.

Speed of first contact is no longer a person's job

Leads assign themselves in seconds. In solar, the first company to call back usually wins the job.

Marketing spend can be judged, not defended

Each lead carries its source for life. Budget moves toward what actually produced sales, with evidence.

Buyers arrive half-sold

Calculators answer the money question and the bot answers the rest, at any hour. Price anxiety is handled before the sales call, not during it.

Owned, not rented

A commercial CRM charges per seat every month, forever, and holds your customer data. All three systems are yours, on your server, and cost nothing extra as the team grows.

The build after that — months 4 to 12

Today the system covers everything up to the moment the customer says yes. The larger half is still to build.

Four tracks, each one a real quarter of work. They can run in any order, and the first is the widest gap in the business today.

Track one

After the customer says yes

Right now the job leaves the system the moment it is won. Everything after that still runs on phone calls and spreadsheets.

  • Jobs and scheduling — site survey, install date, crew and van assigned, all on a calendar tied to the deal.
  • An installer app on the phone — checklist, roof photos, serial numbers and customer sign-off captured on site.
  • Rebate paperwork generated — STC and compliance forms filled from the job record instead of retyped.
  • A customer portal — the buyer logs in to see their quote, contract, install date, documents and warranty in one place.
Track two

Money in, money out

The system knows what you sold. It does not yet know what you made.

  • Contracts with e-signature — signed from the phone, stored against the deal.
  • Deposits, invoices and payment status — visible on the same board as the sale.
  • Finance applications — green loans started from inside the deal instead of a separate portal.
  • Stock and supplier orders — panels, inverters and batteries: what is on hand, what is committed, what needs ordering.
  • Margin per job — cost of goods and labour against the sale price, so you see profit, not just revenue.
Track three

The intelligence layer

The chatbot proved the engine works. The same engine can be pointed at the rest of the business.

  • AI that drafts the follow-up — reads the conversation, writes the next email for the rep to approve.
  • Calls answered out of hours — a voice assistant takes the enquiry, logs it as a lead with a transcript.
  • Design from a map — roof layout, shading and yield estimated from the address, producing a priced system in minutes.
  • Live system monitoring — pull performance from installed inverters to trigger service alerts and battery upgrade offers.
Track four

Reach and repeat revenue

The hardest engineering is done once. Extending it is comparatively cheap.

  • More states — QLD, SA and WA rebate pages from the same generator that made NSW, VIC and ACT.
  • A referral engine — past customers introduce neighbours, tracked and rewarded automatically.
  • Maintenance plans — cleaning, servicing and monitoring sold as a subscription. Recurring revenue from customers you already have.
  • A dealer and partner portal — electricians and resellers submit jobs into your pipeline.

The second revenue line

The chatbot was built as a platform, not a single bot.

One installation can run separate chatbots for many different companies, each with its own documents, branding, phone number and dashboard, fully sealed from the others. That means it can be sold as a monthly service to other businesses without building anything new — the engineering is done. What remains is pricing, sign-up and billing. This is a decision to make, not a project to start.
