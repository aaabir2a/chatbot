# Solar Panel FAQ

## What are the benefits of installing solar panels?

Installing solar panels reduces your electricity bills, decreases dependence on
the grid, increases property value, and contributes to a cleaner environment by
using renewable energy.

## How much money can I save with solar energy?

Savings from solar energy depend on your energy consumption, system size, local
electricity rates, and available sunlight. Most customers see significant
reductions in their monthly electricity costs after switching to solar.

## How long do solar panels last?

Most high-quality solar panels have a lifespan of 25–30 years or more. Solar
panels keep producing electricity beyond this period, although their efficiency
gradually decreases over time.

## Do solar panels work on cloudy or rainy days?

Yes. Solar panels still generate electricity on cloudy or rainy days, though at
a reduced output compared to bright, sunny conditions.

## What maintenance do solar panels need?

Solar panels require minimal maintenance. Regular cleaning and periodic
inspections help ensure optimal performance and a long lifespan.

## How long does solar panel installation take?

A typical residential solar panel installation is completed within 1–3 days,
depending on the system size and site conditions.

## Can solar panels power my entire home or business?

Yes. A properly designed solar system can cover most or all of your energy
needs, depending on your electricity consumption and the available installation
space on your roof.

## What happens to solar panels during a power outage?

Standard grid-connected solar systems automatically shut down during a power
outage for safety reasons. Adding a battery storage system lets your solar
system provide backup power when the grid is unavailable.

## Is my roof suitable for solar panel installation?

Most roofs are suitable for solar panel installation. Suitability depends on the
roof's orientation, angle, shading, available space, and structural condition.
Kratos Energy assesses your roof and designs a system that maximises energy
generation for your specific site.

## If my electricity retailer's feed-in rate is only 8 cents per kWh, is installing solar panels still a worthwhile investment?

Yes, installing solar panels remains a highly worthwhile investment even with a low feed-in tariff of 8 cents per kWh. The primary financial value of rooftop solar in Australia is no longer about earning money from exporting excess power back to the grid. Instead, the real savings come from maximizing your solar self-consumption during the day, which allows you to avoid purchasing expensive mains grid electricity that typically costs several times the feed-in rate.

## How many solar panels do I need to run a standard house in Australia?

The number of solar panels required to run a house depends on your household size, daily electricity usage patterns, and geographic location. A typical single-person home consumes approximately 8 to 12 kWh per day, whereas a larger household of five people with high-draw appliances like a pool and ducted air conditioning can easily consume 30 to 40 kWh per day. While a 6.6 kW system is currently the most common size for Australian homes, larger systems of 9 to 10 kW or more are increasingly popular to support high energy demands, future battery integrations, or electric vehicles.

## I have a flat roof. Should I lay my solar panels completely flat, or are tilt frames necessary?

You should avoid laying solar panels completely flat on a flat roof unless you are prepared to climb onto your roof and clean them manually on a very regular basis. Most solar panels are designed to be installed at a slope of at least 10 degrees, which allows rainwater to naturally wash away accumulated dust, dirt, and debris. Flat-mounted panels allow water to pool on the glass, leaving muddy marks as they dry, which shades the solar cells and severely reduces the system's daily energy generation.

## What can I expect my residential solar system to produce, on average, per day throughout the year?

The average daily energy production of your solar system depends heavily on its total capacity and your location. For a standard, high-quality 6.6 kW system, you can expect an average output of approximately 20 kWh per day over the course of a year. However, actual production is highly seasonal, peaking significantly higher during long, clear summer days and dropping to much lower levels during cloudy winter periods.

## Are solar panels genuinely hail-resistant, and what happens if they are damaged by a severe storm?
All solar panels installed in Australia are built with tempered glass covers that are rapidly cooled during manufacturing, making them 4 to 5 times stronger than regular glass and capable of surviving basic hail impacts. Under mandatory Australian and international standards, panels must pass impact tests using 25mm hailstones traveling at high speeds. However, because some severe Australian storms produce much larger hailstones that can crack even premium glass, you must notify your home and contents insurance provider to confirm that your solar system is fully covered under your policy.
## How does shade affect my solar panels' performance, and what options do I have to mitigate this issue?
Shade from nearby trees, chimneys, or adjacent buildings blocks sunlight and can significantly decrease the overall power generation of a standard string-inverter solar system. To mitigate shading issues, your designer can utilize advanced techniques such as installing DC optimizers or microinverters under each panel, which allows every module to operate independently so that a shaded panel does not drag down the performance of the unshaded panels in the array.
## Do solar panels perform worse on extremely hot summer days, and how do I choose panels that handle the heat?
Yes, solar panels actually lose efficiency and produce less electricity as they heat up, which is a physical characteristic measured by the "Temperature Coefficient of Pmax" on a panel's specification sheet. For every degree Celsius above the standard testing temperature of 25°C, a panel's output degrades by its temperature coefficient; premium panels feature a low coefficient of around -0.3% per degree, whereas inferior panels can degrade by -0.5% or more, making lower coefficients highly desirable for hot Australian summer conditions.
## Is it safe to collect and drink rainwater that is harvested from a roof containing solar panels?
Yes, it is generally safe to collect and consume rainwater harvested from a roof with solar panels. However, because solar panels are exposed to the elements, they naturally accumulate bird droppings, dust, and environmental pollution over time. To ensure water safety and prevent these surface contaminants from entering your drinking supply, it is highly recommended to install a first-flush water diverter and a proper filtration system on your rainwater storage tank.
## Are 25-year performance warranties normal for solar panels, and what is the difference between product and performance warranties?
Yes, a 25-year or 30-year written performance warranty is standard in the solar industry, guaranteeing that the panels will degrade slowly and still produce at least 80% to 85% of their original output after 25 years. However, this is separate from the physical product warranty, which covers actual manufacturing defects, cracking, or equipment failures and is typically shorter (ranging from 10 to 25 years depending on whether you purchase a budget or premium brand); consumers should focus on robust product and installation warranties that are supported by a local Australian office.
## Why did my installer quote a solar panel array that is larger than the capacity of my inverter?
Oversizing your solar panel array relative to your inverter's capacity is a standard and highly effective design practice. Because solar panels rarely operate at 100% of their rated output due to real-world factors like roof angles, temperature degradation, and cloud cover, having a larger panel capacity (such as 6.6 kW of panels on a 5 kW inverter) ensures that your inverter operates at its maximum capacity for a longer portion of the day, maximizing your daily energy yield without damaging the system.
## What additional electrical upgrades might my property require before a solar system can be safely installed?
Depending on the age and condition of your home's electrical infrastructure, you may need to upgrade your main switchboard to accommodate new circuit breakers, install modern residual current devices (RCDs), or replace old wiring to ensure compliance with modern safety standards. Additionally, older mechanical spinning-disc meters must be replaced with digital smart meters by your energy provider before your solar system can be officially switched on and configured for solar billing.
## How can I monitor my solar system's generation and keep track of my actual household energy consumption?
Most modern high-quality solar inverters include a dedicated smart monitoring application that connects to your home's Wi-Fi network to display your real-time solar generation. To monitor your actual household energy consumption alongside your solar production—which is incredibly useful for improving energy efficiency and maximizing self-consumption—you must ask your installer to add a dedicated smart consumption meter to your main switchboard during the installation.
## If I am installing solar panels now but want to add a battery in a few years, what hardware should I choose to prepare my system?
If you plan to add a battery in the future, you should choose a backup-capable hybrid inverter rather than a standard central string inverter, which acts as a "future-proof" foundation that manages both your panels and battery directly when you eventually buy one. Alternatively, you can install a standard solar system now and add an AC-coupled battery later, as AC-coupled batteries connect directly to your home's main AC wiring and integrate seamlessly with any existing inverter brand.
## How do I find a reputable solar installer and what specific accreditations should I verify before signing a contract?
To protect your investment, you should obtain at least three written quotes and verify that your chosen solar retailer is a NETCC-approved seller, which guarantees they commit to high standards of quality in sales, contracts, and customer service. Additionally, you must confirm that the physical installation will be overseen by a technician accredited by Solar Accreditation Australia (SAA), who should present their digital identification card and electrical contractor license on-site on installation day.
## Can I save money by purchasing solar panels and installing them on my roof myself?
No, you should never attempt a DIY solar installation under any circumstances. Installing solar panels and high-voltage electrical cabling is extremely dangerous, requires specialized technical knowledge, and must legally be completed by a licensed electrician who is accredited by Solar Accreditation Australia (SAA). Attempting a DIY install violates Australian building codes and electrical safety standards, voids your equipment warranties, disqualifies you from receiving any government solar rebates, and will void your home insurance coverage.
## How can I ensure that the solar panels quoted by my installer are high-quality and will not fail prematurely?
You can protect your purchase by ensuring that the quoted solar panels are certified on the Clean Energy Council’s (CEC) approved product list and have undergone rigorous independent stress testing. Independent testing laboratories like PV Lab evaluate popular panel brands using standard output tests, visual defect checks, wet leakage tests to measure moisture resistance, and advanced electroluminescence (EL) infrared photography to detect invisible microcracks that degrade performance over time.
## What is a National Metering Identifier (NMI), and why do I need it to set up my solar system?
A National Metering Identifier (NMI) is a unique, 11-digit number assigned to the physical electricity connection point at your property, which can be found on your electricity bill. Your solar installer and electricity retailer require this number to process your solar grid connection application, secure pre-approval for grid exports, and update your smart meter billing structure once your system is fully commissioned.
## My old solar hot water system is failing. Should I replace it with a new solar thermal system, or should I install extra solar panels to power a heat pump?
With solar panels dropping significantly in price, energy experts commonly advise that you will receive better long-term value by skipping a roof-mounted solar thermal water heater and using that roof space to install extra solar photovoltaic (PV) panels instead. These extra panels can then be used to power a highly efficient electric heat pump hot water system, which is significantly cheaper to run than conventional heaters, can reduce water heating costs by 70% to 80%, and leaves you with surplus electricity that can power other household appliances or charge an electric vehicle.
## Will my solar panel installation be delayed if it rains on the scheduled day, and will I be charged extra fees?
Yes, a wet roof is highly slippery and presenting a major safety hazard, meaning that your installation will likely be delayed and rescheduled if it rains heavily on your scheduled installation day. Because electricity and water are a dangerous mix, reputable installers prioritize technician safety, and you should not be charged any additional rescheduling or processing fees if your job is delayed due to wet weather.
## What critical information and safety demonstrations should my accredited solar installer provide before leaving my property on installation day?
Before leaving your property, your accredited installer must perform a thorough, professional system handover that includes showing you how to safely shut down and restart the system for emergency or maintenance purposes, demonstrating how to read the inverter display and navigate the monitoring app, explaining what different inverter warning lights or alerts mean, identifying which household circuits are backed up by the battery, and providing a comprehensive documentation package that outlines warranty details and contact information for technical support.
## What are the primary financial benefits of installing a commercial solar system for my business or commercial building?
Installing a commercial solar system allows your business to generate its own clean electricity directly on-site, significantly reducing your monthly electricity bills by up to 70% and avoiding rising utility charges. Because most commercial operations, machinery, refrigeration, and HVAC systems run continuously during peak daylight hours (8:00 am to 5:00 pm), businesses achieve exceptionally high rates of self-consumption, protecting them from wholesale energy price volatility and significantly improving corporate cash flow.
## How long does it typically take for a commercial solar system to pay for itself, and what factors influence this timeline?
The return on investment (ROI) and payback period for a commercial solar system typically ranges from 2 to 4 years, though some complex systems may take up to 7 years. This rapid payback is driven by your facility's daily electricity consumption patterns, the total size of the solar array, your local utility tariff structure, and the utilization of valuable government incentives such as the federal STC rebate and potential accelerated asset depreciation write-offs.
## Will the installation of a commercial solar system disrupt my daily business or manufacturing operations?
No, an experienced and professional commercial solar installation team will carefully coordinate and structure the project to ensure minimal disruption to your daily operations. Any heavy integration work that requires shutting down your main switchboard for connection and testing is typically scheduled during your business's off-peak hours, over weekends, or completed in planned stages to preserve your operational workflow.
## Can my business claim tax benefits or immediate asset write-offs on a commercial solar system installation?
Yes, commercial solar installations qualify for valuable Australian tax incentives and depreciation benefits that can significantly lower the net cost of your investment. Depending on current tax regulations and your corporate eligibility, your business may be able to claim immediate tax deductions for the cost of the system under the instant asset write-off scheme or utilize accelerated depreciation schedules to reduce your annual taxable income.
## Can my business earn money by exporting surplus commercial solar energy back to the grid, or are there network restrictions?
While some smaller commercial systems can export power, local Distribution Network Service Providers (DNSPs) frequently impose strict export limits or complete zero-export mandates on larger commercial solar arrays to prevent overloading the local grid. For this reason, a reputable commercial solar provider will analyze 12 months of your facility's interval energy data to design and size a system that maximizes on-site self-consumption during the day, rather than relying on grid exports.
## What ongoing maintenance and cleaning are required to keep a commercial-scale solar system performing optimally?
Commercial solar systems are designed for high durability with minimal moving parts, requiring low active maintenance over their 25-to-30-year lifespan. However, to prevent a gradual decrease in efficiency from UV exposure and environmental wear, you should have a licensed electrician conduct comprehensive system inspections every 2 to 3 years to test electrical cabling, check connections, and inspect components for defects; additionally, businesses in high-dust agricultural or manufacturing environments should schedule routine annual panel cleaning to prevent stubborn particulate buildup.
## How do we assess if our business's roof is structurally and environmentally suitable for commercial solar panels?
Before recommending a system size, a professional solar provider will conduct a thorough site assessment that evaluates your building's structural integrity to ensure it can support the additional weight of the mounting rails and panels, analyzes local shading constraints from nearby trees or adjacent structures, and reviews 12 months of actual interval energy data to confirm that your daytime consumption patterns align with solar generation windows.
## What are the Environmental, Social, and Governance (ESG) benefits of installing commercial solar for my company?
Adopting commercial solar demonstrates a tangible corporate commitment to environmental sustainability by directly reducing your company's greenhouse gas emissions and carbon footprint. This transition aligns with global ESG reporting criteria, significantly enhances your brand's reputation, and strengthens relationships with environmentally conscious clients, investors, and community stakeholders who value corporate responsibility.
## If I have access to free daytime grid power from my retailer, is it better to buy a battery to charge from the grid instead of installing solar panels?
While charging a large home battery entirely from the grid during free midday retail windows (typically 11:00 am to 2:00 pm) is an emerging cost-saving strategy, it does not completely replace the long-term financial value of local solar panels. Solar panels generate completely free electricity that directly offsets your consumption outside of those brief free windows and provides an essential charging source during extended blackouts; furthermore, the federal battery rebate requires some form of solar PV to be installed on-site to qualify for the most cost-effective and compliant battery systems.
## How do I scientifically calculate the correct battery storage size for my home?
To size your home battery correctly, you should work with an installer to follow a structured, three-step scientific process: first, analyze your smart meter data to determine your average night-time energy consumption from sunset to sunrise, which represents the primary load your battery needs to replace; second, ensure your rooftop solar array is large enough to reliably charge the battery even during gloomy winter days; and third, decide on a reserve safety level (typically 10% to 20%) to keep locked away specifically for unexpected grid blackouts.
## Can I retrofit a battery to my existing solar panel system, or do I need to replace my existing inverter?
You do not need to replace your existing solar panel setup or inverter to add storage. You can perform an AC-coupled retrofit, which involves installing a separate battery system with its own integrated battery inverter that connects directly to your home's main AC electrical wiring, allowing it to work seamlessly alongside any existing string or microinverter solar system.
## Should I wire my home battery to back up my entire house during a blackout, or should I only back up essential circuits?
For the vast majority of households, it is far more practical and cost-effective to set up a partial backup system that isolates and powers only your "essential" circuits—such as your refrigerator, basic lighting, internet router, and a few power outlets to charge devices—during an outage. Whole-home backup is technically possible but requires massive, highly expensive multi-phase battery and inverter stacks, as high-draw appliances like ducted air conditioning and pumps draw startup surge currents that can easily exceed standard inverter limits and trip the entire system.
## Why are grid-tied solar systems and standard batteries designed to shut down completely during a power outage?
Standard solar inverters and grid-tied batteries are programmed with automatic safety shutdown protocols (known as anti-islanding protection) that activate the moment they detect a grid outage. This shutdown is legally mandated to prevent your solar system from exporting high-voltage electricity back into the street power lines, which would pose a fatal shock risk to utility technicians who are physically working to repair the damaged grid infrastructure.
## Will my rooftop solar panels continue to generate electricity and charge my battery during a blackout?
Whether your panels can charge your battery during an outage depends entirely on your system's design and inverter capabilities. Many basic battery retrofit systems do not support solar recharging while the grid is offline, meaning that once you drain the battery's stored energy, you are left without power until the utility grid returns. To ensure your solar panels continue generating power and recharging your battery during a prolonged blackout, you must choose a hybrid inverter system configured with true "islanding" or grid-forming backup capabilities that physically isolate your home from the grid.
## Does my property require a three-phase hybrid inverter and battery system if my home has a three-phase grid connection?
No, you do not necessarily require a three-phase hybrid inverter just because your home has a three-phase connection, as a single-phase hybrid inverter can operate on a single phase and utilize net metering across all phases to offset your energy costs. However, during a blackout, a single-phase inverter can only supply backup power to the specific circuits physically wired to that single phase; if you want the ability to run backup power across appliances connected to different phases of your home, you will need to install a true three-phase hybrid inverter.
## What is the difference in physical and electrical efficiency between AC-coupled and DC-coupled battery installations?
DC-coupled battery systems are slightly more electrically efficient because the DC power generated by your solar panels is sent directly into the battery for storage through a single, streamlined path. In contrast, AC-coupled systems require the solar energy to travel through your solar inverter to be converted to AC power, and then travel through a battery inverter to be converted back to DC power to charge, resulting in a dual conversion efficiency loss of approximately 5% to 10%.
## What is Ausgrid’s "Sun Tax" (two-way solar tariff), and will I have to pay a fee to export my excess solar energy?
Ausgrid’s two-way solar tariff is a pricing structure designed to encourage solar owners to consume their self-generated power during peak daytime hours or shift exports to the evening. Under this tariff, you are charged a small fee of 1.23 cents per kWh for exporting excess solar electricity to the grid during the peak generation hours of 10:00 am to 3:00 pm, but you are rewarded with a credit of 3.85 cents per kWh for exporting power back into the grid during the evening peak demand window of 4:00 pm to 9:00 pm.
## How does the monthly "free export threshold" prevent me from being charged under the new two-way solar tariff?
You are not charged for every single watt of solar you export during the middle of the day, as the tariff includes a generous monthly "free export threshold" of approximately 200 kWh (calculated as roughly 6.8 kWh per day, varying slightly between 192 and 212 kWh depending on the length of the month). You are only charged the 1.23 cent per kWh fee on the excess volume of daytime solar exports that exceeds this monthly threshold, meaning the actual financial impact on a typical residential 5 kW solar owner is estimated to be less than $7.00 per year.
## What practical strategies can I use to avoid paying the daytime solar export tax entirely?
You can easily avoid or minimize two-way solar export charges by adopting three practical self-consumption strategies: first, set timers or utilize home automation to run high-draw appliances like dishwashers, pool pumps, washing machines, and ducted air conditioners during the middle of the day when your panels are generating peak power; second, install a solar hot water diverter or a smart EV charger to absorb excess generation on-site; and third, install a home battery system configured to store your daytime solar generation so you can use or export it after 4:00 pm to earn credits.
## Does the mandatory "Sun Tax" export charge apply to solar owners in Victoria, Queensland, or other Australian states?
No, the mandatory "Sun Tax" export charge has not been adopted nationwide. It is currently in place only in New South Wales and South Australia under network operators like Ausgrid, Essential Energy, and SA Power Networks. Other state governments, including Victoria, have actively blocked the enforcement of mandatory solar export charges, with Victorian distributors like Ausnet and United Energy stating they have no plans to introduce any mandatory solar tax before July 2031.
## What is the federal "Cheaper Home Batteries" program, and how does it lower the upfront cost of home storage?
The Cheaper Home Batteries program is a federal subsidy administered under the Small-scale Renewable Energy Scheme (SRES) that provides an upfront discount of roughly 25% on the purchase and installation of eligible battery systems. To qualify, your battery must have a nominal capacity between 5 kWh and 100 kWh, be listed on the Clean Energy Council (CEC) approved product list, and be installed by a technician accredited by Solar Accreditation Australia (SAA); the installer will automatically apply for the rebate and deduct its value directly from your upfront invoice.
## Why does the federal battery rebate discount decrease per kilowatt-hour for larger battery systems?
The federal battery rebate uses a sliding, tapered scale to incentivize moderate, household-sized energy storage systems that align with typical residential needs. Under this scale, the first 14 kWh of usable capacity receives the maximum subsidy of $271 per kWh, capacity between 14 and 28 kWh receives a reduced rate of $162 per kWh, and capacity from 28 to 50 kWh drops sharply to $40 per kWh (with no rebate applied beyond 50 kWh), meaning a standard 10 kWh battery receives a highly cost-effective $2,720 rebate while a massive 40 kWh system is capped at $6,560.
## Can I claim the federal battery rebate on a secondary dwelling like a granny flat, tiny home, or caravan?
Yes, you can claim a separate federal battery rebate on a secondary dwelling, provided that the structure is actively lived in, is completely separate from the main residence, and has its own independent solar PV system installed. To qualify, the battery must remain installed for the duration of its warranty or until the end of 2030, and you must provide your installer with clear photographs of the dwelling, a map showing its physical separation from the main house, and the vehicle identification or hull number if it is a mobile dwelling like a caravan.
## How is the Victoria Solar Homes rebate eligibility changing on July 1, 2026, and what is the deadline to apply under the current rules?
Effective July 1, 2026, the combined household taxable income eligibility cap for the Victoria Solar Homes program is dropping from $210,000 per year down to $150,000 or less per year. If your household's combined income falls between $150,000 and $210,000, you must act quickly and submit your completed application with all required identity and income documents via the Service Victoria portal before 5:00 pm on June 30, 2026, to secure the $1,400 solar PV rebate and matching interest-free loan.
## How can New South Wales residents access upfront battery discounts of up to $2,400 under the Peak Demand Reduction Scheme (PDRS)?
To access upfront battery discounts of up to $2,400 under the NSW PDRS, you must be a resident of New South Wales, own an eligible rooftop solar system, and purchase a newly manufactured battery with a nominal capacity between 2 kWh and 28 kWh. You must coordinate the purchase and installation through an IPART-approved Accredited Certificate Provider (ACP), who will complete the compliance checks, have you sign a nomination form, and deduct the rebate value directly from your total invoice.
## Is joining a Virtual Power Plant (VPP) worth the financial returns, or will the extra charging cycles wear out my battery too quickly?
Joining a Virtual Power Plant (VPP) involves a trade-off where you allow a central operator to occasionally discharge your battery during peak grid shortages in exchange for upfront discounts or ongoing credits. While this extra cycling does add a small amount of wear to the battery cells, premium batteries are designed with robust lifecycles similar to electric vehicles and are guaranteed to maintain 60% to 85% capacity after 10 years regardless of standard VPP use; for most households, VPP participation is a low-stakes decision that helps stabilize the grid while providing a modest ongoing credit.
## What is the difference between a Virtual Power Plant (VPP) and a Virtual Energy Network (VEN) for a home battery owner?
The primary difference lies in who controls your hardware and how your electricity is traded. A Virtual Power Plant (VPP) aggregates and centrally controls your battery, with the operator deciding when to charge or discharge your system to support the grid in exchange for fixed financial benefits. A Virtual Energy Network (VEN) is an alternative platform that does not control your battery; instead, it acts as an open, peer-to-peer trading network that charges a monthly fee to let you dynamically buy and sell energy with other users or trade directly on the wholesale spot market.
## How can I prevent my home battery from discharging its stored energy to charge my electric vehicle?
You can prevent your residential battery from draining into your electric vehicle by setting up smart coordination rules within your system's energy management application. High-quality inverters and smart EV chargers (EVSE) communicate with each other, allowing you to prioritize your household loads first, utilize only "surplus" daytime solar generation to charge your car, and block the EV from pulling power from the home battery bank.
## What is a solar hot water diverter, and is it a better financial option than standard off-peak controlled-load hot water?
A solar hot water diverter is a smart energy management device that monitors your home's electricity flow and automatically redirects any excess solar energy that would otherwise be exported to the grid into your conventional electric hot water system. This effectively turns your hot water tank into a highly cost-effective thermal battery, which is significantly cheaper and offers better long-term financial value than purchasing increasingly expensive off-peak controlled-load power from the grid at night.
## Why did my solar battery fail to turn back on and charge from the sun after completely draining to zero during a power outage?
If your solar battery drains to absolute zero during an extended blackout, it loses the residual "brain power" required to run its internal computer systems and activate the inverter when the sun rises. When a battery is completely dead, it cannot perform its daily automated routine to "look" for solar power, meaning you may have to pay for a technician to perform a manual "jump start" once the grid returns; to prevent this, you should always configure your battery's backup settings to reserve at least 5% of its capacity to ensure the system can safely wake up and recharge from solar the next morning.
## Can I clean my rooftop solar panels myself, and can I use a high-pressure washer to remove stubborn dirt?
You can perform basic visual checks and gently sweep away loose debris from the ground using a long-handled, soft-bristled broom if your home is a single-story property. However, you must never use a high-pressure washer, harsh soaps, or abrasive chemicals on your panels, as extreme pressure can create microcracks in the tempered glass and ruin the protective anti-reflective coating; if your panels are highly soiled, installed flat, or difficult to reach, you should hire a qualified professional to ensure safety and prevent voiding your manufacturer’s warranty.
## How often should my residential solar system be professionally serviced, and what does this service involve?
To ensure your solar system remains safe, highly efficient, and structurally secure, you should have it professionally serviced by a licensed electrician who is accredited by Solar Accreditation Australia (SAA) at least once every 2 years. A comprehensive professional service involves performing deep cleaning, clearing debris from underneath the panels, testing electrical components and wiring connections, inspecting cables and roof-mounted isolator switches for UV degradation, and checking the inverter's fault history, heatsinks, and ventilation grilles.
## Do solar panels naturally clean themselves when it rains, or do I still need to clean them?
Yes, solar panels that are installed on a standard pitched roof with an angle of 10 degrees or more will naturally "self-clean" as rainwater washes away standard accumulations of dust, pollen, and light debris. However, if your panels are installed completely flat on a flat roof without a tilt frame, rain will pool on the glass and leave muddy marks as it evaporates, requiring manual cleaning or a gentle hose-down to maintain optimal performance.
